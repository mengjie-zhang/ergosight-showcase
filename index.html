The challenge

Musculoskeletal injuries are costly, common and largely preventable.

Work-related musculoskeletal disorders build up quietly through repeated awkward postures, heavy handling and long static holds. In European industry they account for more than half of all work absences. Yet the way risk is assessed has barely changed.

Slow

Observational assessments take an expert hours per task, so only a small sample of work ever gets checked.

Subjective

Different assessors score the same task differently, and a single snapshot can miss the moments that matter most.

Impractical on site

Wearable sensors and motion-capture labs are accurate but intrusive, expensive and hard to use on a live worksite.

Built for real workplaces

Messy sites, moving cameras, whole crews. No problem.

Real workplaces are not laboratories. Views are blocked, people move around, cameras are handheld and work happens at height. ErgoSight is designed for these conditions. Each clip below shows the original footage next to ErgoSight's 3D reconstruction.

No tripod needed

Handheld footage, straight from the camera

Filmed by hand on site: the camera shakes, the worker crouches behind a rebar cage and the head is partly out of frame. ErgoSight still rebuilds the full crouching posture in 3D, follows it smoothly and scores every frame.

Why it matters: any supervisor's phone becomes an assessment tool. No equipment to set up, no fixed camera positions, and any task at any location can be captured in seconds. That is what makes risk screening scalable across many sites.

Heavy occlusion

Sees past what blocks the view

A worker kneels behind a colleague and a loader bucket, and others are cut off by machinery and the frame edge. ErgoSight infers the hidden body parts and reconstructs a complete, plausible posture for each person.

Why it matters: on construction sites and factory floors, machines, materials, workbenches and co-workers block the view all the time. A tool that only works with a clear, full-body view would miss much of the real work.

Many people at once

Whole crews in busy surroundings

A crew clearing a drain on a busy street, with people in the background and a passer-by walking right in front of the camera. Every worker is followed individually and analysed at the same time, even when briefly hidden.

Why it matters: one video covers a whole team instead of one person at a time. Each worker gets an individual risk profile, so supervisors can see who is most exposed.

Height and distance

Workers at height, seen from the ground

A crane lift on a building under construction, photographed from street level. Workers on the roof edge and the scaffold are small and far away, yet each one is detected and reconstructed in 3D.

Why it matters: work at height is where getting close with equipment is hardest and where posture matters most. ErgoSight can assess it from a safe distance.

Attention & teamwork

Know where your team is looking.

Safe, productive work depends on attention as much as on posture. From ordinary video, ErgoSight estimates each worker's line of sight and what they are looking at, without eye-tracking glasses or extra hardware. Teams can see shared focus, attention shifts and who is looking where.

Hazard awareness

Check whether workers look at the hazards that matter, or only at the most obvious ones.

Team coordination

Measure shared attention during briefings, lifts and handovers, where everyone needs to look at the same thing.

Distraction & workload

Spot frequent attention switching and periods when focus is pulled away from the task.

Training & supervision

Compare how experienced and new workers scan a task, and turn that into targeted coaching.

Informed by research: construction workers' visual attention patterns are linked to how many hazards they identify (Hasanzadeh, Esmaeili & Dodd, 2017, J. Construction Engineering and Management). Shared gaze can make collaborative visual search about twice as efficient (Brennan et al., 2008, Cognition). Advances in estimating gaze targets from ordinary video (Recasens et al., 2015; Chong et al., 2020) make this possible without wearable eye trackers.

How it works

Four steps. No special equipment.

Record

Film the task with a phone or an existing site camera. Workers wear nothing extra and work as normal.

Upload

Add the video to ErgoSight. Processing can run on your own computer, so footage need not leave your organisation.

Review

Explore each worker in 3D, see risk by body part over time, follow attention, and add context such as load weight or grip.

Act

Prioritise the highest-risk tasks, test changes and compare before and after to show the improvement.

Benchmarked against motion capture

Close to the lab reference. On lifting tasks, ErgoSight followed body posture far more closely than a widely used open-source pose tool. Trunk bending stayed within a few degrees of laboratory motion capture.
